\bigskip

\noindent The orior transpiler moves an operation from one language to another through one intermediate
form. A symbol of a source language maps to a bridge word of the transpiler. The bridge word maps to an anchor
of the ladder, and the anchor is a relation on machine words. The relation's cases are the operation's gsm, the
relational assembly of gnascor. A second table maps the bridge word back out to a symbol of the target language.

This research paper states that transform as a composition of finite maps and proves each of its parts in Lean
4. The proofs cover three faces. The ladder relations hold on every case that ladder.h states, at every width and
in both readings of the sign. The matrices of gsm obey five laws over all 86 of their rows. Each operator of
\texttt{.g}, the high order face, compiles to the gsm of one anchor, for every pair of words of every width.

Each language joins the atlas as a chart: a table and a Lean file of its own. The table names the bridge word of
each symbol. The Lean file proves that the symbol's semantics, by its own standard, is the anchor's relation, and
Lean accepts no chart without that proof. A Lean theorem then proves that any two symbols of any two sound charts
that reach one anchor agree, at every width and sign. The theorem covers the 1:1, 1:many, many:many and n:many
projections. \texttt{.g} is a sound chart at every width and sign, and C and Python are sound charts on
\texttt{uint32\_t}. Each chart is fail-closed, and a symbol outside it halts. A defect legend adds the known
typos, idioms and 1:1 mappings of a dirty corpus, disjoint from the chart and closed on its targets. Lean proves
that the legend keeps every meaning, and that it reports each unknown symbol with its place. A language check
fails where a witnessed symbol lacks its theorem, and a lift refuses every symbol that has no proof.
