\section{The permutation-null method on Salishan}

This chapter applies the method of the delta null research paper \cite{src:Quigg-delta-null} to the Salishan papers: it finds the language in a paper, and decides what may join a gold standard corpus.

\subsection{The idea}

English is the target. The object of study is what English does not account for.

That turns the problem around, onto the half that is well resourced. Nobody has to know Nsyilxcən or Lushootseed to find it in a paper. English has to be known, and the residual text is the language, a gloss, or a page of font damage.

\subsection{The procedure}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Build a gold standard corpus for each language from the hand extractions.
\item Build an English reference corpus from the translations the hand extractions mark.
\item Screen each paper against the reference corpus, and keep what English does not account for.
\item Measure what was kept against each language's profile, and admit it only where the measurement says it can be read (Section 3).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The components}

The measurement reduces a text to its byte-pair histogram, a bigram distribution \cite{src:Shannon-1948}, and compares two texts by the total variation distance between their histograms. A language profile is the histogram of one language's gold standard corpus, in the sense of $n$-gram language identification \cite{src:Cavnar-and-Trenkle-1994}. Each profile carries a split-half distance, the distance between two halves of its own corpus, and a comparison between corpora has to beat it to be read. The measurement has no tuned parameter and no term for any one language. Every distance, verdict and median below is an exact rational, computed in integer arithmetic on the engine's record machine (\texttt{gold\_readings.cu} in the orior repository).

Around it sit three other components: an automatic extractor written for each of \Extractors{} papers, with that paper's repairs and checks; the screening applied to papers with no extractor, together with the dialect border test; and the word web, which joins the forms of every hand extraction by concept, shape and context.

\subsection{Results}

These results are over \HandExtractions{} hand extractions. Of these, \Extractors{} have an automatic extractor, and every language token of \ExtractorsAccounted{} of those is accounted for:

\begin{itemize}
\item \HandExtractions{} of \HandExtractions{} papers have a hand extraction;
\item \ExtractorsAccounted{} of \Extractors{} papers have every language token accounted for;
\item \GoldLanguageLines{} lines of gold standard target language, across \GoldLanguages{} languages.
\end{itemize}

The \NoExtractor{} with no extractor, Hilbert and Hess \cite{src:Hilbert-and-Hess-1975} among them, are read by hand, and they are held ungraded and not counted. The \GoldLanguages{} languages are the ones with a gold standard corpus.

There are \GoldProfiles{} profiles: \GoldLanguages{} languages and English. Of the \GoldPairs{} pairs, \GoldPairsReadMidpoint{} are readable, at distances \GoldPairLeast{} to \GoldPairMost{} against split-half distances of \GoldSplitLeast{} to \GoldSplitMost{}. The pair that does not read is \GoldUnreadLeft{} against \GoldUnreadRight{}, \GoldUnreadDistance{} apart with the \GoldUnreadWider{} profile's own split-half distance at \GoldUnreadSplit{}.

That split-half distance is not the resolution of the estimator at \GoldUnreadWiderLines{} lines. Cutting a corpus at its midpoint measures the order its papers are in: a corpus is its papers concatenated, the cut lands on a paper boundary, and it reports the distance between whichever papers it separates. Lushootseed is two papers in two dialects and two orthographies, and the midpoint puts one on each side. Sampling the halves by alternating puts a mixture on both sides, and the split-half distance falls to \GoldUnreadAlternating{}. Every corpus moves the same way, by \GoldRatioLeast{} to \GoldRatioMost{} times, and the midpoint figure is the larger every time. A resolution cannot rise when its sample grows, and the midpoint figure does. The split-half distances are therefore sampled by alternating.

Read at the alternating split-half distances, all \GoldLanguagePairs{} pairs among the \GoldLanguages{} languages separate, \GoldUnreadLeft{} against \GoldUnreadRight{} included, and \GoldLanguagePairsChanged{} of the \GoldLanguagePairs{} change verdict. The \GoldPairs{} above counts the same \GoldLanguages{} languages plus English.

The papers are a separate question, and the profiles' split-half distances do not decide it. Each paper is judged against its own split-half distance. Of the \GoldPapers{} papers naming a language the profiles cover, \GoldPapersRead{} are readable, and on \GoldPapersAgreed{} of them the distributions agree with what the paper's prose says the language is. Of the \GoldPapersNotRead{} that are not readable, \GoldPapersSmall{} hold too small a sample to ask, fewer than \GoldPapersLeast{} byte pairs, and \GoldPapersUnread{} fail for one reason: the gap between the nearest profile and the runner-up has a median of \GoldMedianGap{}, against a median paper split-half distance of \GoldMedianSplit{}. The gap is \GoldNoiseTimes{} times inside the noise.

Screened from the papers with no extractor:

\begin{itemize}
\item \SiftPapers{} papers screened;
\item \SiftNearer{} lines nearer the language, and \SiftResidue{} lines of residual text for a person to review;
\item \SiftNamed{} of the \SiftPapers{} carry the language their own front matter names.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What it cannot do}

Support is still climbing on every one of these corpora. None has seen its own alphabet. A candidate that looks nothing like a member is not thereby wrong, and resembling what is already there is the wrong test.

The screening finds candidates. It does not read them. The corpus is still hand extracted and verified in three passes, for line and for notation, and the screening says where to look.

Growing the Lushootseed profile does not reach the \GoldPapersNotRead{} papers. The \GoldPapersUnread{} that are large enough to ask fail because a paper's residual text sits almost the same distance from all \GoldLanguages{} language profiles: the median gap between the nearest and the runner-up is \GoldMedianGap{}, and the median paper split-half distance is \GoldMedianSplit{}. Nothing about the profiles' own sizes moves those two numbers past each other.

What the \GoldPapersUnread{} need is a measure that separates the profiles as seen from a mixed residual, which the byte-pair histogram does not. The dialect border section of the chapter \emph{How wrong it could be} gives the first one that does: holding a concept fixed with the word web's gloss edge, flattening the pooled counts to maximum entropy, and testing one run at a time. That recovers a published dialect border inside Lushootseed at a corpus size where the whole-distribution comparison needs \BorderNeeds{} times more text. It has not been applied to the \GoldPapersUnread{}.

Two dialects pooled into one profile are still worth splitting, because pooling inflated the split-half distance from \GoldUnreadAlternating{} to \GoldUnreadSplit{}, and it is the same defect in every corpus that holds more than one paper. Splitting them corrects the profiles; it is not a route to the \GoldPapersUnread{}.
