# What magic costs

This family established what is *cheap*. Nothing yet said what the expensive thing costs.

The cheap side is settled and measured: a perfect matching across a cut of 4096 qubits holds 2048
bits of entanglement in a megabyte; Clifford gates conserve magic to 6.66e-16; the T gate is exactly
free on the exact simulator, at distance 0, because the state carries an exact inverse root of two,
and not a float. The exponential does not live in the entanglement: the Bell state
disproves that.

Where does it live? **Stabilizer rank.**

Write a state as a sum of k stabilizer states and a Gottesman-Knill style simulation runs in time
proportional to k, because each term is separately cheap. The minimum such k, written χ, therefore
*is* the classical simulation cost of that state. Not a proxy for it.

## No tolerances, and that is forced

χ answers "is this vector in the span of those". A span membership decided by a float comparison is
a span membership decided by a threshold somebody picked, which this tree does not allow for
anything that decides a result.

It does not need one. The work drops normalization throughout: χ is a question about spans, and a span
does not care about scale. That keeps every amplitude an algebraic integer in **Z[ζ₈]**, the ring
generated by ζ = e^(iπ/4):

| unnormalized | value | lives in |
|---|---|---|
| H | [[1, 1], [1, -1]] | Z |
| S | diag(1, i) | i = ζ² |
| CZ | diag(1, 1, 1, -1) | Z |
| \|T⟩ | (1, ζ) | the only place ζ itself enters |

Linear algebra over the degree-4 field Q(ζ₈) then decides membership exactly. A rank comes out an
integer or the machinery is broken; there is no third outcome to tune.

## The enumeration carries the weight of the check

Closure under H, S and CZ generates the stabilizer states, and the count must match
2ⁿ∏(2ᵏ+1) exactly. An *absent* stabilizer state would make every χ too large:

| qubits | states found | formula |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 6 |
| 2 | 60 | 60 |
| 3 | 1080 | 1080 |

With a positive control, where a stabilizer state must have χ = 1, and a negative one: χ can never
exceed 2ⁿ, because the computational basis states are themselves stabilizer states and span the whole
space. 11 checks, 0 failures.

## The result

| T states | χ | how |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **2** | full search, complete |
| 2 | **2** | full search, complete |
| 3 | **3 ≤ χ ≤ 4** | lower bound exhausted, upper bound constructed |

**Two T states still cost only two terms.** The naive 2ᵐ is not what magic costs, and χ holding flat
from one T state to two is the sub-multiplicativity that makes magic-state simulation tractable at
all. It breaks at three: the pair search over all 1080 stabilizer states runs out without a hit, and
χ(|T⟩³) ≥ 3. **The growth starts there, located exactly, and not asserted.**

## The witnesses, which a rank on its own is not

"χ is 2" is a claim about a search. "χ is 2 and here are the two states and the two coefficients,
and they add back exactly" is a claim a stranger can check without rerunning anything. Douglas asked
for precisely this: *return the set that lets others reproduce it.*

**One T state.** |T⟩ = (1, ζ):

> (1 + ζ)·|1⟩ + 1·(|0⟩ − |1⟩) = (1, ζ)

**Two T states.** |T⟩⊗|T⟩ = (1, ζ, ζ, ζ²):

> ζ·(|01⟩ + |10⟩) + 1·(|00⟩ + i|11⟩) = (1, ζ, ζ, i), and ζ² = i

A Bell state plus |00⟩ + i|11⟩. Both terms are stabilizer states, both coefficients are exact, and
it is checkable by hand in one line.

## The upper bound is free and still verified

χ(ψ⊗φ) ≤ χ(ψ)·χ(φ), because if ψ = Σaᵢsᵢ and φ = Σbⱼtⱼ then ψ⊗φ = Σaᵢbⱼ(sᵢ⊗tⱼ), and a tensor
product of stabilizer states *is* a stabilizer state. Then χ(|T⟩³) ≤ 2·2 = 4, with no search at all.

It comes from the two witnesses above, and not from an argument: the 4 terms rebuild |T⟩³ **exactly**,
and all 4 are confirmed to be genuine 3-qubit stabilizer states by lookup in the enumerated 1080.

The exact value inside 3 ≤ χ ≤ 4 is **open**. The triples number 209 million, and nobody enumerates them.
The value therefore stays open, and nobody guesses it.
